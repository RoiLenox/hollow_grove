# The Runtime Federation V1

Date: 2026-07-28

Status: canonical Hollow Grove architecture and implementation milestone

Stable identity: `runtime-federation.hollow-grove.v1`

## Canonical name

The shared operational continuity of Hollow Grove is called:

# **The Runtime Federation**

The Runtime Federation joins accepted results from the universal recursion
kernel, constitutional runtime, House authorities, world systems, canonical
calendar, routes, Functions, Synthesis, permanence, gameplay, and later
presentation without erasing the identity or authority of any one of them.

It is the architecture through which distinct lawful runtimes remain distinct
while participating in one continuing world.

Its canonical statement is:

> **The kernel computes. The Houses authorize. The Runtime Federation carries
> lawful consequence forward. Gameplay asks. Presentation witnesses.**

The Runtime Federation is not:

- a fifth House;
- a second Hollow Grove Constitution;
- a government or sovereign;
- a new universal kernel;
- a Great Function;
- Central Junction;
- Function Junction;
- a replacement for a domain reducer;
- permission for one archive to rewrite another archive's history;
- a presentation client capable of declaring canonical state.

## Kernel canon

The universal recursion kernel remains pure and completes one bounded pass.
It may expose neutral identity, composition, scale, Point, polarity,
orientation, and provenance. It does not know Houses, Central Junction,
Functions, seasons, routes, awards, gameplay, or The Runtime Federation by
proper name.

The Runtime Federation begins only after a kernel pass has completed. It may
retain a stable reference to that completed result as evidence, but it may
neither reopen the pass nor change what the pass computed.

Continuity therefore belongs to the federation boundary:

```text
completed universal kernel pass
    ↓
constitutional interpretation and authority
    ↓
domain-owned accepted events
    ↓
The Runtime Federation
    ↓
one continuing canonical world
```

The four Hollow Grove phases are kernel-facing constitutional grammar, not new
universal-kernel variants:

```text
The Way Back
    → The Initiation
    → The Gathering
    → The Festival
    → The Way Back
```

The Way Back carries the prior confirmed state forward. The Initiation gives
it actionable form. The Gathering places that form into relationship. The
Festival determines what became true. What became true enters The Way Back
again.

Return is re-entry with consequence, not restoration to an untouched starting
state.

## Constitutional position

The Runtime Federation sits above the universal kernel and constitutional
domain reducers and below authoritative gameplay orchestration:

```text
universal recursion kernel
    ↓
Hollow Grove constitutional runtime
    ↓
House and shared-interface runtimes
    ↓
The Runtime Federation
    ↓
authoritative gameplay runtime
    ↓
transport protocol
    ↓
later presentation clients
```

The Hollow Grove Compromise remains the only shared Hollow Grove Constitution.
The four House constitutions retain their sovereign domains. The Runtime
Federation coordinates accepted records; it creates no new authority.

Each participating runtime remains responsible for:

- validating its own commands;
- identifying its own events;
- preserving its own provenance;
- rejecting unlawful state;
- encoding and migrating its own domain history;
- replaying its state through its own production reducer.

The Federation may validate relationships between accepted domain records. It
may not reinterpret a House decision, manufacture missing evidence, grant
Title, issue Clearance, prove a Design, apply Recog, execute Synthesis, seal
Permanence, decide Tournament victory, or transfer sovereignty.

## Lore meaning

Hollow Grove does not continue because every part of the world agrees on one
meaning. It continues because different meanings can meet without losing
their source.

The Runtime Federation is the constitutional memory of that coexistence. It
remembers:

- which Point or completed pass supplied a fact;
- which Being, object, Bond, route, Function, or year was affected;
- which House acted;
- which authority allowed the action;
- which evidence supported or opposed it;
- which domain accepted or rejected it;
- which consequences reached gameplay;
- which state The Festival confirmed;
- what must return through the next Way Back.

In ordinary civic language, a person may say that an event has "entered the
Federation" when its domain authority, evidence, provenance, and canonical
result have been accepted into the continuing world record.

That phrase does not mean the event was approved morally, rewarded, made
permanent, or owned by a central institution. Rejections, injuries, failures,
violations, unresolved discrepancies, and lawful refusals may all enter the
Federation as true history.

## Federation laws

### 1. Law of retained identity

Every component, event, subject, and result uses a stable caller-supplied
identity. Federation identity must never derive from vector position,
insertion order, rendering order, current catalog contents, map coordinates,
random generation, or client state.

### 2. Law of domain custody

A domain record remains under the reducer and constitutional authority that
created it. Federation does not convert coordination into ownership.

### 3. Law of causal reference

Cross-domain relationships use explicit stable references and causal
positions. Copying display text is not a constitutional relationship.

### 4. Law of accepted consequence

Only accepted domain events may alter canonical state. Rejected attempts may
retain evidence and history but cannot falsely produce their intended result.

### 5. Law of exact replay

Federated loading must replay every component through its production
validation boundary and reproduce the same final state. A checksum alone is
not proof of constitutional validity.

### 6. Law of contradiction

A valid checksum cannot make contradictory records lawful. Missing
dependencies, mismatched subject identity, reversed route direction,
unauthorized permanence, automatic Recog, sovereignty transfer, or
cross-domain history rewriting must fail closed.

### 7. Law of canonical order

Serialization order is deterministic and derived from stable identities and
explicit causal positions. Reversing insertion order must not change identity,
digest, replay, or outcome.

### 8. Law of migration

Every supported archive migration is explicit, versioned, idempotent where
appropriate, provenance-preserving, and incapable of silently duplicating a
cycle, event, subject, or Function.

### 9. Law of presentation non-authority

Rendering, animation, camera direction, sprite state, audio, user-interface
order, or network arrival order cannot create, skip, reverse, or validate a
canonical transition.

### 10. Law of constitutional non-sovereignty

Federation participation grants no House, institution, client, champion,
Bearer, Keeper, operator, or archive permanent sovereignty over Central
Junction, another House, or Hollow Grove as a whole.

## Canonical federation manifest

The implementation milestone must create one versioned, deterministic
federation manifest. The suggested archive identity is:

```text
archive.runtime-federation.hollow-grove.v1
```

The suggested archive format is:

```text
HGRF
```

The manifest must record at minimum:

- federation identity and archive version;
- ruleset identity;
- canonical-year identity;
- component archive identities;
- component kinds, formats, versions, and digests;
- component dependencies;
- stable domain-event references;
- causal positions;
- current Grove phase;
- physical and digital checkpoint identities;
- accepted result state;
- prior confirmed state;
- evidence and authority references;
- provenance;
- migration history;
- deterministic aggregate digest.

A component archive remains independently verifiable and replayable. The
federation archive binds components; it does not flatten them into one
ambiguous event enum.

At minimum the first canonical manifest must bind:

- the constitutional runtime archive;
- the regional Synthesis archive where present;
- institutional authority state;
- world-Point and world-binding state;
- the seasonal and Function Junction archive;
- the Service Tournament archive nested beneath The Gathering;
- route and passage state;
- permanence state;
- authoritative gameplay history.

## Runtime behavior

The Runtime Federation must support:

- component registration by stable caller-supplied ID;
- dependency validation;
- domain-event indexing;
- subject and evidence cross-reference inspection without inventing ownership;
- exact component replay;
- exact aggregate replay;
- deterministic canonical serialization;
- checksums or digests;
- tamper detection;
- supported migrations;
- idempotent loading;
- rejection of missing, duplicate, cyclical, or contradictory dependencies;
- operational replay recovery;
- read-only audit and inspection.

Federation should add event-ID-to-index projections where profiling shows
linear lookup to be a replay bottleneck. An index is a derived optimization,
not a new source of identity or truth.

## First playable proof

The first complete consumer of The Runtime Federation should be one contained
Central Junction operation rather than the whole city-wide Tournament.

It must demonstrate:

1. The Way Back loading prior confirmed consequence.
2. The Initiation creating a lawful operation or candidate state.
3. The Gathering placing that state into world relationships.
4. The Festival accepting or rejecting the result.
5. One rejected attempt that preserves evidence without false mutation.
6. One accepted result that becomes the next Way Back input.
7. One real-emergency distinction.
8. One cross-domain causal reference.
9. One constitutional restraint decision.
10. One permanence petition requiring all four House proofs.
11. Replay to the exact same final state.
12. Presentation rendering only accepted runtime results.

The operation may use the Service Tournament and The War of a Thousand Hues,
but it must remain contained, nonlethal, and subordinate to The Gathering.

## Persistence migration target

The authoritative gameplay archive should advance from schema V2 to a
federation-aware schema V3 only when the federation manifest and replay
boundary are executable.

Migration must preserve V1 and V2 gameplay histories, institutional snapshots,
stable event identities, House decisions, Bonds, party state, route
consequences, and refusals. Old saves must not acquire seasonal, Tournament, or
permanence events that they never contained. Compatibility defaults must be
explicitly identified as migration decisions rather than inferred from
display state.

## Audit requirements

A dedicated Runtime Federation audit must verify and print:

- federation identity and archive version;
- universal-kernel purity;
- component archive identities and supported versions;
- dependency integrity;
- component checksums;
- aggregate checksum;
- canonical-year identity;
- current Grove phase;
- fixed four-phase loop;
- world Point binding;
- Function Junction and House Season state;
- Tournament nesting and nonlethality;
- route direction and body/force/route distinctions;
- permanence proof and Stonebend seal rules;
- gameplay archive linkage;
- rejection history without false state mutation;
- deterministic replay;
- insertion-order independence;
- presentation non-authority;
- no sovereignty transfer.

The audit must return a failing status when any invariant is violated.

## Implementation boundary

This canon establishes the name, purpose, architecture, invariants, archive
target, and first playable proof of The Runtime Federation.

It does not by itself claim that the HGRF archive, gameplay schema V3, unified
inspection tools, or Central Junction operation have already been implemented.
Those capabilities become canonical runtime facts only when production code,
migrations, fixtures, audits, and tests pass and are committed at a separate
provenance boundary.

Presentation work remains downstream. Godot may render accepted Federation
state but may never define it.
