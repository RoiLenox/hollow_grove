The supplied c. 1900–1600 BCE coordinate is retained as a Sequential Point, but its assignments are not treated as equal chronological blocks. Babylon is Full / Transforming: its First Dynasty spans approximately 1894–1595 in the Middle Chronology, but hegemony is late and brief. Minoan Crete is Full / Transforming across the Protopalatial and early Neopalatial phases near c. 1700. Old Assyria is a Full period label with Uneven evidence: the conventional label reaches c. 1600, while the richest Kültepe archive ends much earlier.

## Historical uncertainties

- Babylon: absolute dates vary by chronology; early Babylon was not yet hegemonic; the Hammurabi monument is not a modern comprehensive code; Old Babylonian archives from other cities are not evidence for one Babylonian administration.
- Middle Kingdom Egypt: the exact Dynasty 13 and Second Intermediate boundary remains disputed; Itjtawy is unlocated; private monuments are socially selective; literary speakers, authors, composition dates, and witnesses remain distinct.
- Minoan Crete: `Minoan`, `palace`, Protopalatial, and Neopalatial are modern analytical labels; the c. 1700 destruction horizon has no single proven cause; scripts remain undeciphered or interpretively limited; territorial government is not recoverable.
- Old Assyria: evidence is temporally concentrated; Assur itself is archaeologically difficult for the period; network geography and jurisdiction varied; `kārum` must not be flattened into modern colonial sovereignty.
