## Chronology decision

The supplied c. 1200–900 BCE coordinate is retained as a Transition Point, but its four labels describe different kinds of fit. `Kingdom of Israel` is People full / Kingdom emergent: the name Israel is externally attested around 1208, while highland communities, disputed early monarchy, and the northern kingdom occupy distinct evidence layers. `Phoenicia` is Full regional continuity / Emergent label: independent coastal cities continue while a shared external category and inscriptional profile emerge unevenly. `Aram` is Emergent / Dispersed: locally distinct communities and polities precede or accompany a sparse written corpus. `Neo-Assyria` is Boundary / Eleven-year slice: only c. 911–900 belongs conventionally to the period.

No supplied label, date, assignment, or order was rewritten. The dossier layer constrains what each label may support.

## Historical uncertainties

- Kingdom of Israel: Iron I–IIA dates, the historical scale and unity of early monarchy, and the relation between later literary narrative and archaeological evidence remain disputed.
- Phoenicia: the umbrella term, dates of early Byblian inscriptions including Ahiram, and the timing and structure of western expansion require city- and source-specific treatment.
- Aram: Ahlamu, Aramaean, and Aram are not interchangeable; local polity formation and the earliest Old Aramaic texts cluster near or after the boundary and cannot define one people or material style.
- Neo-Assyria: some periodizations begin with Ashur-dan II in 934 rather than Adad-nirari II in 911; royal annals are biased, and later relief, siege, provincial, and communication systems cannot be backdated.
