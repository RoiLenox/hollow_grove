## Chronology decision

The supplied 336–281 BCE range remains a Transition Point, not a synchronized four-state tableau. `Achaemenid` is Political 336–330 / Institutional legacy thereafter: Darius III and Achaemenid sovereignty survive the 336 opening but end during Alexander's conquest, while administrative forms and local institutions outlive the dynasty. `Macedonia` is Full horizon / Referent transforms: the label moves from the Argead kingdom through Alexander's composite empire into successor claims and a contested Macedonian homeland.

`Ptolemaic` is Egyptian precursor 332–323 / Satrapy 323–305 / Kingdom 305–281, so it cannot be backdated as a kingdom to 336. `Seleucid` is Precursor 323–315 / Formation 312–305 / Kingdom 305–281, with Seleucus's recovery of Babylon in 312 and royal title from 305 treated as formation thresholds rather than one instantaneous beginning. The 281 boundary marks the deaths of Lysimachus and Seleucus and the close of the first successor generation; it does not imply universal stabilization.

## Historical uncertainties

- Achaemenid: the exact collapse of central authority, local accommodation, archive survival, administrative continuity, and the relationship between Alexander's claims and Achaemenid precedents vary by region and source.
- Macedonia: the term changes political referent across the Point; literary narratives are later and partisan, successor legitimacy was contested, and foundation or renaming claims need local archaeological and epigraphic control.
- Ptolemaic: early court, temple, fiscal, military-settlement, language, and collection histories are unevenly documented; later Library narratives cannot be projected uncritically into its formation.
- Seleucid: the 323–312 precursor phase, territorial control, army size, settlement policy, regional autonomy, and continuity from Achaemenid or Babylonian institutions require local and chronological qualification.
