## Chronology decision

Point XI remains the supplied 180–313 CE coordinate. It is a comparison window, not one synchronized “crisis and conversion” event. Roman political reconstruction, Christian community growth, Alexandrian intellectual continuity and disruption, and Iranian dynastic change follow different phases.

The supplied Flynt assignment remains **Partial**: Sasanian rule begins in 224 CE. The reviewed dossier labels 180–224 as late Parthian, then separates Ardashir, Shapur I, and later early Sasanian phases. It does not backdate Sasanian institutions to 180.

The 311 and 313 toleration measures form the terminal legal transition. They do not make Christianity the Roman state religion in this Point and do not prove one uniform early Church.

## Historical uncertainties

- “Third-century crisis” was not uniform across Roman provinces; local law, municipal life, labor, infrastructure, fiscal burden, and recovery require separate evidence.
- Early Christian sources are partial and argumentative; later institutional unity, orthodoxy, and continuous persecution cannot be projected backward without audit.
- Alexandrian schools and collections changed. The Ptolemaic Library, Roman-era collections, philosophical teachers, Christian schools, medical traditions, and later curricula must remain distinct.
- Royal Sasanian inscriptions and Roman narratives are interested witnesses; early centralization, local authority, religious policy, and exact frontier chronology remain contested.
