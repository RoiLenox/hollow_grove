## Chronology decision

The supplied c. 900–722 BCE range remains a Sequential Point. It is the first Atlas coordinate in which all four assignments have substantial contemporary state or institutional evidence, but their endpoints are asymmetric. `Israel` is Full / Terminal and means the northern kingdom centered on Samaria. `Phoenicia` is Full / Continues and remains a field of independent cities and linked settlements. `Aram` is Full regional horizon / Uneven terminal because individual polities were annexed at different dates and Hamath's final annexation lies around 720. `Assyria` is Full / Continues and is operationally Neo-Assyrian despite the supplied shorter label.

The supplied fall of Samaria remains the terminal event. The dossier does not force a single reconstruction of conquest between Shalmaneser V, Sargon II, the Babylonian Chronicle, later royal claims, biblical accounts, and archaeology.

## Historical uncertainties

- Israel: the precise 722/720 sequence, the roles of Shalmaneser V and Sargon II, the scale and timing of deportation, and the relation of royal inscriptions, biblical accounts, and settlement evidence remain debated.
- Phoenicia: the umbrella term does not erase city-specific government, Assyrian relations, or uneven western settlement histories; the provenance and date of individual objects require case-level review.
- Aram: no single state, ethnicity, or art style covers the coordinate. Arpad, Damascus, Sam'al, Hamath, and other polities require local treatment, and later Imperial Aramaic cannot be backdated.
- Assyria: royal texts are curated claims; administration changed across expansion, civil war, magnate dominance, revolt, plague, reform, and succession. The Point ends before Sargon II's mature system.
