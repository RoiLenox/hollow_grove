## Historical uncertainties

- Akkad: Agade is unlocated; royal territorial claims are not a reliable map of uniform administration; many inscriptions survive only in later copies.
- Old Kingdom Egypt: the Point corresponds to the late Old Kingdom and its terminal transition, not the full period; elite mortuary evidence is socially selective.
- Mature Indus: the script remains undeciphered, government and named beliefs are unknown, and regional urban variation resists a single-city template.
- Awan: the center is unlocated, most evidence is adversarial Mesopotamian writing, the treaty is difficult, and Puzur-Inshushinak falls at or just after the Point boundary.

## Outstanding research questions

- Which Old Akkadian administrative archives best distinguish local continuity from royal centralization?
- How should Sandmanor represent non-elite Old Kingdom memory when the surviving record is dominated by royal and elite mortuary contexts?
- Which Mature Harappan water features have the clearest maintenance evidence at each major city?
- Can new archaeological or philological work narrow Awan's geography without conflating it with all Elam, Susa, or the western Iranian plateau?
