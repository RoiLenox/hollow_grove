# Historical Dossier — Byzantine Empire, Justinianian Continuity, and Frontiers

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

“Byzantine” is retrospective; inhabitants and officials generally understood themselves as Roman. Laws, chronicles, coins, buildings, papyri, church records, and archaeology represent court, urban, provincial, and frontier perspectives unevenly.

## Historical overview

The eastern Roman state continued after 476. Anastasius stabilized finances; Justinian's reign pursued legal compilation, building, diplomacy, and reconquest in Italy and Africa; later rulers faced Persian and Balkan pressure. Continuity and restoration claims must be separated from war cost, regional autonomy, and local change.

## Chronology

476 western succession; 491–518 Anastasius; 527–565 Justinian and Theodora; 529–534 legal compilation and African/Italian campaigns; 540s–550s plague and war; 568 Lombard settlement in Italy; 602–610 crisis before Heraclius.
