# Historical Dossier — Judea and Early Christianity under Roman Rule

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary
Josephus, early Christian texts, Jewish inscriptions, papyri, archaeology, and Roman references are partial, partisan, and differently dated.
## Historical overview
Judean communities and early Christian movements developed amid Roman rule, Jewish diversity, diaspora networks, and contested temple and civic authority. Christianity was not yet uniform.
## Chronology
27 BCE–14 CE Herodian and Augustan settlement; 14–66 provincial and sectarian development; 66–73 revolt and temple destruction; 73–180 diaspora and early Christian diversification.
## Major figures
Herod, Jesus, Paul, Josephus, priests, teachers, early Christian leaders, governors, and diaspora communities.
## Major cities
Jerusalem, Galilee, Caesarea, Antioch, Alexandria, Rome, Asia Minor, and diaspora settlements.
## Government
Herodian and Roman administration, priestly institutions, councils, synagogues, households, and emerging assemblies held distinct authority.
## Religion
Jewish law, temple practice, apocalyptic expectation, sectarian interpretation, diaspora identity, and early Christian proclamation interacted.
## Economy
Temple finance, taxation, pilgrimage, agriculture, estates, craft, labor, and trade structured unequal communities.
## Trade
Grain, textiles, coin, crafts, travel, letters, and pilgrimage linked Judea and the Mediterranean.
## Science
Scripture interpretation, rhetoric, healing traditions, calendrical practice, correspondence, and translation circulated through teachers.
## Technology
Roads, ports, writing materials, household meeting spaces, water systems, and couriers enabled movement and community formation.
## Architecture
Temple, synagogue, household, tomb, road, and civic forms expressed overlapping identities.
## Military
Occupation, revolts, temple destruction, and local conflict shaped memory; later Christian triumph is not backdated.
## Art
Inscription, ritual object, manuscript, synagogue decoration, and symbolic language developed locally.
## Language
Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, and Latin operated in different communities and administrations.
## Major texts
Josephus, Pauline letters, other early Christian texts, Jewish writings, inscriptions, and papyri.
## Major discoveries or innovations
The inheritance is networked correspondence, portable interpretation, multilingual community organization, and memory under pressure.
## Historical relationships
Communities engaged Rome, Parthia, Egypt, Syria, Greece, and one another through debate, mission, diplomacy, conflict, and exchange.
## Permanent Gifts
- Judean identity and religious memory
- early Christian community networks
- Roman civic and diplomatic translation
