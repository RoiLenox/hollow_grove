# Historical Dossier — Ptolemaic Egypt, Late Dynasty and Roman Pressure

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working; reviewed dossier continuing Points VII–VIII.

## Evidence boundary
Greek and Demotic papyri, temple inscriptions, contracts, petitions, coinage, archaeology, and Roman narratives are unevenly distributed and must not be collapsed into one royal archive.

## Historical overview
Ptolemaic Egypt continued through dynastic conflict, Upper Egyptian revolt, Roman intervention, and Cleopatra VII's final reign. Greek, Egyptian, Jewish, and other communities used overlapping institutions.

## Chronology
146–116: Ptolemy VIII and dynastic conflict; 116–80: competing royal lines and regional administration; 80–51: late dynastic consolidation and Roman pressure; 51–30: Cleopatra VII, Caesar, Antony, Actium, and annexation.

## Major figures
Ptolemy VIII, Cleopatra II, Ptolemy IX, Ptolemy XII, Cleopatra VII, Caesar, Antony, Egyptian priests, Alexandrian scholars, physicians, petitioners, farmers, and soldiers.

## Major cities
Alexandria, Memphis, Thebes, the Fayum, Delta ports, temples, villages, Cyprus, Cyrene, and Red Sea stations.

## Government
Royal court, provincial offices, temples, military settlers, local courts, tax offices, and households negotiated authority and service.

## Religion
Egyptian temple traditions, Greek civic religion, royal cult, Jewish communities, and philosophical schools coexisted in translation and tension.

## Economy
Land, taxation, grain, labor, temple estates, military settlement, coinage, and Nile management structured unequal obligations.

## Trade
Grain, papyrus, textiles, spices, gold, luxury goods, and information moved along Nile, Red Sea, African, Aegean, and Roman routes.

## Science
Alexandrian mathematics, astronomy, medicine, geography, textual scholarship, and multilingual administration remained major inheritances.

## Technology
Canals, irrigation, ships, harbors, papyrus administration, surveying, temple construction, medicine, and agricultural storage linked state and community.

## Architecture
Temples, ports, canals, libraries, workshops, houses, and Alexandrian civic buildings combined local and Hellenistic forms.

## Military
Dynastic revolt, Roman intervention, Alexandrian conflict, naval operations, Actium, and annexation disrupted services and communities.

## Art
Temple relief, royal portraiture, coins, papyri, sculpture, and funerary practice joined Greek and Egyptian forms without one fusion style.

## Language
Greek, Demotic, hieroglyphic, and other community languages operated in distinct but overlapping institutions.

## Major texts
Papyri, temple decrees, tax records, petitions, medical and mathematical works, scholarship, and Roman accounts.

## Major discoveries or innovations
The period's inheritance is multilingual institutional preservation: papyrus administration, medical teaching, astronomical calculation, translation, and ecological service management.

## Historical relationships
Egypt engaged Rome, Parthia, Judaea, Cyprus, Cyrene, Nubia, Greek cities, and local institutions through diplomacy, scholarship, commerce, war, and service.

## Permanent Gifts
- Ptolemaic service continuity under Roman pressure
- Alexandrian scholarship and medical preservation
- war displacement and community recovery records
