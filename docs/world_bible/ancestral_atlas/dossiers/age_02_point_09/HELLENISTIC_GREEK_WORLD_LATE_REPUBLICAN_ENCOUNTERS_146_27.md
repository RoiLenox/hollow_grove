# Historical Dossier — Hellenistic Greek World, Late Republican Encounters

Status: Working; reviewed dossier. Atlas assignment remains Working. The umbrella label is deliberately bounded.

## Evidence boundary
Greek cities, leagues, sanctuaries, schools, and communities remained diverse after 146 BCE. Roman and later literary sources are not equivalent to local civic experience.

## Historical overview
Roman provincial administration, client kingship, civic benefaction, philosophical debate, migration, and cultural translation reshaped Greek communities without making Greek life passive survival.

## Chronology
146–133: Roman settlement and local negotiation; 133–88: provincial and civic reordering; 88–63: Mithridatic wars; 63–44: Pompeian and Caesarian interventions; 44–27: civil-war and Augustan transition.

## Major figures
Polybius, Posidonius, Mithridates VI as interlocutor, civic envoys, philosophers, physicians, rhetors, artists, merchants, and displaced residents.

## Major cities
Athens, Rhodes, Delos, Delphi, Corinth, Pergamon, Alexandria, Antioch, and Aegean and eastern Mediterranean ports.

## Government
Poleis, koina, monarchies, client rulers, Roman governors, councils, assemblies, courts, and sanctuaries held differing authority.

## Religion
Civic cults, sanctuaries, festivals, ruler honors, mystery traditions, philosophical ethics, Jewish and Egyptian communities, and Roman ritual overlapped.

## Economy
Benefaction, tax, debt, slavery, estates, coinage, credit, and market exchange connected unequal communities.

## Trade
Grain, wine, oil, metals, books, art, labor, and information moved through ports, sanctuaries, households, and imperial routes.

## Science
Medicine, mathematics, astronomy, geography, rhetoric, philosophy, textual criticism, and teaching circulated through schools and libraries.

## Technology
Harbors, roads, theaters, gymnasia, walls, temples, ships, coinage, papyri, and water systems supported public life.

## Architecture
Theater, gymnasium, sanctuary, agora, harbor, and civic-inscription environments varied by city and patron.

## Military
Roman provincial wars, Mithridatic conflicts, piracy, civic faction, siege, enslavement, and displacement altered communities.

## Art
Sculpture, portraiture, mosaics, theater, architecture, inscriptions, and books served local, royal, Roman, and private patrons.

## Language
Koine Greek connected institutions while local languages and multilingual communities remained active.

## Major texts
Polybius, Diodorus, Plutarch, philosophical and theatrical works, civic decrees, treaties, and sanctuary inscriptions.

## Major discoveries or innovations
The period's durable inheritance is institutional: textual criticism, civic schooling, diplomatic record, medical teaching, and networked cultural exchange.

## Historical relationships
Greek communities engaged Rome, Pontus, Ptolemaic Egypt, Parthia, Judaea, local dynasts, and one another through diplomacy, patronage, education, commerce, and war.

## Permanent Gifts
- civic identity under oligarchic and popular conflict
- rhetorical persuasion and political memory
- cross-cultural diplomacy in a changing Mediterranean

## Modern Hollow Grove manifestation
Republican Memory and Rhetoric Atelier: a Sandmanor theater-archive comparing speech, audience, civic honor, translation, omission, and counter-memory.
