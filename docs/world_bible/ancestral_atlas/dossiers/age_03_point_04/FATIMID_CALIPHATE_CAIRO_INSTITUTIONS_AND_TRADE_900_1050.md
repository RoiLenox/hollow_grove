# Historical Dossier — Fatimid Caliphate, Cairo Institutions, and Trade

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Historical overview

The Fatimid caliphate conquered Egypt in 969 and founded Cairo as a political center while retaining and adapting older Egyptian, Mediterranean, Red Sea, and Islamic institutions. Isma'ili authority coexisted with Sunni, Christian, Jewish, and local communities.

## Chronology

909 Fatimid foundation in Ifriqiya; 969 conquest of Egypt and Cairo foundation; al-Azhar; tenth- and eleventh-century Nile, Mediterranean, and Red Sea trade; 996–1021 al-Hakim; 1036 al-Mustansir begins, beyond the Point's main terminal emphasis.

## Permanent Gifts

- Cairo institutions and urban administration
- intellectual pluralism and scholarly patronage
- Mediterranean and Red Sea trade networks

## Modern Hollow Grove manifestation

Cairo Institutions and Exchange Forum: a Sandmanor setting comparing urban office, religious community, scholar, market, port, and local reception without importing caliphal authority.
