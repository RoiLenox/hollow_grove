# Historical Dossier — Sasanian Persia, Mature Frontier, and Institutions

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

Royal inscriptions and reliefs, coins, archaeology, Syriac and Armenian texts, later Persian and Arabic histories, and Roman sources preserve different audiences and agendas. Sasanian administration was layered; royal ideology does not prove uniform control or a single religious experience.

## Historical overview

The fourth and fifth centuries saw mature Sasanian kingship, repeated Roman wars, frontier negotiation, noble and provincial power, urban and trade continuity, and changing relations among Zoroastrian, Christian, Jewish, Manichaean, and other communities. Shapur II's long reign and later fifth-century rulers belong to distinct phases.

## Chronology

309–379 Shapur II; 363 Jovian settlement after Julian's defeat; fourth-century frontier war and Christian community pressure; 387 Roman-Sasanian partition of Armenia; 420s–450s conflicts and negotiated settlements; 459–484 Peroz and the eastern frontier crisis.
