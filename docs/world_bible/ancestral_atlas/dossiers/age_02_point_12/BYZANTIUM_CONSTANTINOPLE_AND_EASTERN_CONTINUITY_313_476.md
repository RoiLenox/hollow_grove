# Historical Dossier — Byzantium, Constantinople, and Eastern Continuity

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

“Byzantium” is a retrospective label for the eastern Roman empire; contemporaries generally understood themselves as Romans. Court histories, laws, church records, archaeology, coinage, papyri, and urban evidence reveal continuity and conflict, not a seamless Greek successor state.

## Historical overview

Constantinople became an imperial center after 330, joining Roman law, taxation, army, court, Christian patronage, and Mediterranean trade to an older eastern urban world. Eastern administration, cities, and frontier diplomacy continued after western fragmentation, while internal religious conflict and fiscal inequality remained real.

## Chronology

330 dedication of Constantinople; 395 administrative division; fifth-century eastern court and provincial continuity; 451 Chalcedon; 457 Leo I; 474–476 eastern recognition and management of western succession.
