# Historical Dossier — Imperial Christianity, Councils, Institutions, and Conflict

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

Councils, creeds, letters, sermons, inscriptions, archaeology, imperial laws, martyr narratives, and later ecclesiastical histories are partisan and regionally uneven. “Imperial Christianity” does not mean one church, one doctrine, or universal conversion.

## Historical overview

Imperial patronage after Constantine expanded Christian building, episcopal networks, charity, and public debate while traditional cults, Judaism, local practice, dissent, and non-Christian institutions persisted. Nicaea and later councils produced contested formulations; emperors, bishops, cities, monasteries, households, and communities negotiated authority differently.

## Chronology

313–324 toleration and patronage; 325 Nicaea; fourth-century Trinitarian and Christological disputes, imperial interventions, ascetic and monastic growth; 380 Theodosian legislation; 381 Constantinople; 431 Ephesus; 451 Chalcedon; fifth-century regional disagreement and institutional differentiation.
