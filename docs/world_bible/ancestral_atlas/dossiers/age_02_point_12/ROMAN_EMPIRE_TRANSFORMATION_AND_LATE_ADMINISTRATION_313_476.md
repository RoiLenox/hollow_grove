# Historical Dossier — Roman Empire, Transformation, Administration, and Western Terminal

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

Imperial laws, papyri, inscriptions, coins, archaeology, panegyrics, Christian histories, and later chronicles preserve different perspectives. “Christian Empire” and “fall” are analytical labels, not uniform experiences.

## Historical overview

After the civil wars ending in 324, Constantine and his successors reorganized imperial court, army, law, taxation, and public religious patronage. The empire remained plural and contested. The eastern center strengthened while western authority fragmented through the fifth century; 476 marks a western imperial succession event, not the disappearance of Roman institutions or people.

## Chronology

313–324 toleration, civil war, and Constantine's consolidation; 325 Nicaea; 330 Constantinople's dedication; 337 succession and division; 378 Adrianople; 395 permanent administrative division; fifth-century western military and fiscal contraction; 476 deposition of Romulus Augustulus and rule continuing through eastern imperial institutions.

## Permanent Gifts

- distributed civic administration under changing sovereignty
