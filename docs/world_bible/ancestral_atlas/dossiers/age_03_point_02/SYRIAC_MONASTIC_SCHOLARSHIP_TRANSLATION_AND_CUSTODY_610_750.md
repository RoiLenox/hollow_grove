# Historical Dossier — Syriac and Monastic Scholarship, Translation, and Custody

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

Syriac, Greek, Arabic, Armenian, Coptic, and Persian texts survive through schools, monasteries, churches, courts, and households with uneven custody. Later Abbasid translation narratives should not be projected backward as a single seventh-century program.

## Historical overview

Syriac Christian communities and monastic institutions preserved, translated, commented on, and taught biblical, philosophical, medical, and scientific materials across Byzantine, Sasanian, and early Islamic settings. Translation was local, multilingual, and shaped by patrons, doctrinal divisions, and practical needs.

## Chronology

seventh-century schools of Edessa, Nisibis, and the Persian Church; monastic and episcopal copying under new rulers; 680s–700s Arabic administrative and scholarly transitions; eighth-century translation activity developing toward but not completing the Point.

## Permanent Gifts

- translation practice with source and language custody
- manuscript preservation and variant comparison
- cross-language scholarship and teaching networks

## Modern Hollow Grove manifestation

Multilingual Custody and Translation Conservatory: a Glaüshouse program that records source language, translator, witness, variant, material condition, access, and later reception.
