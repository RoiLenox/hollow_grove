## Evidence boundary

Tang official histories, epitaphs, manuscripts, legal texts, ceramics, tomb art, coins, archaeology, and frontier records preserve court, military, religious, and urban perspectives unevenly. “Cosmopolitan” must not erase coercion, hierarchy, or local communities.

## Historical overview

The Tang dynasty (618–907) rebuilt imperial institutions after the Sui, expanded and negotiated frontier relations, and connected Chang'an and other cities to Central Asian, steppe, Korean, Japanese, Indian, and West Asian networks. Buddhism, Daoism, Confucian governance, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, and merchant communities coexisted under changing policy.

## Chronology

618 Tang foundation; 626–649 Taizong; 649–683 Gaozong and empress Wu's rise; seventh-century Silk Road and frontier administration; 712–756 Xuanzong, with the An Lushan rebellion just beyond the supplied 750 terminal emphasis.
