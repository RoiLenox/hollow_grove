# Historical Dossier — Byzantine Empire, Heraclian Transformation, and Icon Debates

Status: Working; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

Imperial law, chronicles, coins, seals, church texts, archaeology, papyri, and Arabic and Syriac accounts preserve different regions and agendas. “Byzantine decline” is not a sufficient historical explanation.

## Historical overview

Heraclius inherited a state under extreme Sasanian pressure, recovered much territory by 628, and then faced Arab-Muslim expansion, fiscal contraction, military reorganization, and changing provincial structures. Constantinople and eastern Roman institutions persisted while Syria, Egypt, Palestine, and North Africa changed political hands. The first iconoclast controversy begins after this Point's 750 boundary.

## Chronology

610 Heraclius; 614–619 Sasanian conquests; 622–628 Byzantine counteroffensive and peace; 634–642 Arab conquest of Syria, Palestine, and Egypt; seventh-century reorganization and themes; 717–718 Constantinople siege; 726/730 icon policy begins toward the terminal boundary.
