## Review boundary

- The title, date range, assignments, order, and Cyrus-captures-Babylon
  terminal event remain supplied Atlas data.
- The common 586 BCE date for Jerusalem's destruction is retained in Atlas
  navigation while the dossier exposes the scholarly 587/586 convention.
- `Judah` after 587/586 means differently situated communities and records,
  not an uninterrupted kingdom or a claim that all inhabitants were exiled.
- Naukratis is treated as an Egyptian multi-community port, not simply a
  Greek colony or an image of frictionless cosmopolitanism.
- Neo-Babylonian royal building is studied alongside temple, family,
  business, scholarly, labor, deportee, and maintenance records.
- The political scale of a Median `empire`, its borders, administration,
  material culture, and Herodotean dynasty remain historically disputed.
- Siege, destruction, deportation, assigned labor, imperial extraction,
  foreign-service romance, and conquest are evidence or cautions—not Gifts.
