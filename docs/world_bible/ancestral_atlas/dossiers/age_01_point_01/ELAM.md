# Elam / Proto-Elamite Horizon — Historical Dossier

Civilization: Elam, with Point I evidence centered on the Proto-Elamite horizon<br>
Atlas assignment: Age I, Point I, Flynt<br>
Date relevance: c. 3200–2500 BCE<br>
Status: Reviewed Proto-Elamite benchmark; Atlas assignment remains Working and Needs historical audit and date refinement

## Evidence boundary

**Historical evidence** includes Proto-Elamite tablets and numerical systems,
seals and sealings, storage and public buildings, walls, workshops, ceramics,
metal and stone objects, and settlement archaeology centered on Susa, Anshan,
and Iranian plateau sites.

**Scholarly interpretation** applies “Proto-Elamite” to a writing system and
archaeological horizon around c. 3050–2900 BCE. The script is only partly
understood, its language is unknown, and it is not proven to record an early
form of the later Elamite language.

**Hollow Grove adaptation** takes logistics, accountable custody, dispersed
craft, and storage. It does not invent named rulers, military doctrine, or
translations.

## Historical overview

The Atlas label “Elam” is broader than the best evidence for the first part of
this Point. Around c. 3200–2900 BCE, Susa and centers across the Iranian
plateau used numerical and Proto-Elamite administrative systems and shared
parts of a distinctive material horizon. Anshan grew into a major walled
settlement with public buildings, storage, and craft activity. After c. 2900,
Proto-Elamite tablets and associated practices disappeared from many sites.
Historically Elamite polities and the Awan dynasty become clearer only later,
near or after the end of Point I.

The dossier therefore preserves the supplied culture name while stating that
no single unified “Elamite Empire” spans 3200–2500 BCE.

## Chronology

- **Susa II / late Uruk interaction, before and around c. 3200 BCE:** strong
  Mesopotamian material connections at Susa before renewed plateau influence.
- **Proto-Elamite / Susa III and Banesh horizon, c. 3100–2900 or 2800 BCE:**
  administrative script, seals, distinctive art and ceramics, and a broad
  network of plateau centers.
- **Post-Proto-Elamite transition, c. 2900–2600 BCE:** disappearance of the
  script and major changes in settlement and material practice; evidence is
  regionally uneven.
- **Earliest Old Elamite / Awan horizon, from around c. 2600 BCE:** historically
  named dynasties become relevant near the Point boundary and receive a
  separate Atlas assignment in Point II.

Dates differ by site and publication. “Proto-Elamite” is not a chronological
synonym for every community on the Iranian plateau.

## Major figures

No securely named Proto-Elamite ruler, official, or author can be read from the
script. A large sealed account may have belonged to a powerful household, but
the identity and title of its authority remain uncertain. Named rulers of Awan
belong to later evidence and must not be imported into the early dossier.

## Major cities

- **Susa:** principal findspot for the largest Proto-Elamite tablet corpus and a
  long-lived lowland center with changing Mesopotamian and plateau connections.
- **Anshan / Tal-e Malyan:** major highland settlement with a city wall, public
  mud-brick buildings, storage, painted walls or vessels, and craft activity.
- **Tepe Sialk:** plateau center with Proto-Elamite tablets and associated
  material.
- **Tepe Yahya:** southeastern center participating in administrative and craft
  networks.
- **Shahr-i Sokhta and other eastern sites:** evidence for the wide use or
  movement of related numerical and writing practice.

The network does not by itself prove political control from one capital.

## Government

Tablets, headings, totals, seal control, receipts, transfers, rations, labor,
grain, livestock, storage, and public buildings demonstrate organized
institutions or large households. Their office names and constitutional
relationships are unknown. Later Elamite titles are not valid substitutes.

## Religion

Animal and human seal imagery, special buildings, figurines, and ritual objects
may bear religious meaning. The administrative corpus contains no deciphered
literature or theology. Later Elamite gods and cults should not be projected
wholesale into the Proto-Elamite horizon.

## Economy

The tablets concern grain, livestock, labor, rations, receipts, transfers, and
other institutional accounts. Agriculture and herding intersected with pottery,
stone, metal, textile-related, and other crafts. Lowland and highland ecologies
supported different resources and production.

## Trade

Materials and practices moved among Susiana, Fars, the Zagros, central and
southeastern Iran, and Mesopotamian-facing lowlands. Stone and metal from the
highlands, agricultural products, livestock, crafted goods, and administrative
techniques participated in these connections. Exact routes and political
control must be established object by object.

## Science

Proto-Elamite numerical systems, commodity signs, totals, document layout, and
accounting procedures are the strongest knowledge evidence. Partial semantic
understanding comes from formal and contextual comparison, not a deciphered
language. This is sophisticated administration under a severe interpretive
limit.

## Technology

Clay tablets, numerical notation, sealing, storage, mud-brick public
construction, city walls, ceramics, stone work, metal objects, and workshop
production supported dispersed centers. The record does not warrant a universal
technical system or named engineering profession.

## Architecture

At Anshan, excavations indicate a city wall, precisely built mud-brick public
structures, painted walls, a large building with central storage, and domestic
craft areas. Susa supplies public and administrative contexts but suffered from
early excavation methods that complicate reconstruction. No replica palace is
authorized by this evidence.

## Military

Walls provide evidence of bounded or defended settlement, not a reconstructable
army. Later Mesopotamian-Elamite warfare and Awan rivalry belong to later
Points. Flynt’s reviewed inheritance is logistical capacity rather than an
invented Proto-Elamite martial culture.

## Art

Distinctive glyptic imagery includes animals acting in human roles, alongside
painted ceramics, figurines, stone vessels, and metal objects. Seal imagery
participated in administration as well as representation. Its motifs should not
be copied as unexplained Flynt decoration.

## Language

The Proto-Elamite script is only partly deciphered. The language behind it is
unknown, and a genetic relationship to the later Elamite language has not been
demonstrated. The script must remain unread in Hollow Grove displays unless a
real published reading is cited with confidence.

## Major texts

- Administrative receipts, transfers, ration accounts, grain and livestock
  records, and labor-related tablets.
- Numerical tablets that precede or sit outside the strict Proto-Elamite class.
- Sealed tablets and sealings that connect document, household, and custody.

There are no known Proto-Elamite literary or school-text corpora comparable to
later Mesopotamian traditions.

## Major discoveries or innovations

The important inheritance is a linked operational system: numerical accounting,
seals, stores, craft, and exchange could coordinate resources across distinct
ecological zones. The disappearance of the writing system is also a caution
against assuming that every technical standard persists or becomes legible to
later cultures.

## Historical relationships

Susa sat at a lowland interface where Mesopotamian and plateau practices changed
in strength over time. Anshan and other highland centers contributed a broader
network. Similar script does not prove one ethnicity, language, or centralized
state, and “Elam” should remain a reviewed umbrella label at this Point.

## House interpretation

Flynt asks what a system can do under pressure. Point I answers through
logistics: maintain custody, move necessary material across terrain, coordinate
production and stores, and admit what cannot be read. The present constitutional
law is the **Law of Capacity**. Authority remains with the
[Flynt Constitution](../../../../../FLYNT_CONSTITUTION_V2.md), Tross, Wardens,
Manticorp, and other established structures. Recognition may remain a cultural
or confirmation theme; it is not the House Law.

### Archaeological evidence

Susa stratigraphy and tablet corpus; Anshan walls, public buildings, central
storage, painted features, domestic craft; plateau tablets and sealings;
ceramics, stone and metal objects, and settlement survey. Early excavation
quality and old provenience records must be documented.

  bibliography and corpus history.

## Research notes

- The supplied “Elam” label should be retained but marked as a broad ancestry
  label spanning a major archaeological and historical transition.
- Proto-Elamite is a script/horizon term, not a proven language or ethnicity.
- The script is partially understood structurally, not fully deciphered.
- Susa and Anshan should not be collapsed into one permanent capital system.
- Awan belongs in Point II; named later rulers and campaigns remain excluded.
- “Frontier” in the manifestation means modern route and supply conditions, not
  an unsupported ancient frontier ideology.
