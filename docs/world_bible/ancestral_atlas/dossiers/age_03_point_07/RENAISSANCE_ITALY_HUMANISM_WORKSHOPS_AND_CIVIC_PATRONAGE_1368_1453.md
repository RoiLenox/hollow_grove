# Historical Dossier — Renaissance Italy: Humanism, Workshops, and Civic Patronage, 1368–1453

Status: Reviewed; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

“Renaissance Italy” names a plural comparison among republics, oligarchies,
signorie, duchies, papal territories, courts, guilds, religious institutions,
and cities. Florence is important but does not stand for the whole peninsula.
Elite authorship and patronage never erase assistants, artisans, laborers,
women, poorer residents, religious minorities, war, or exclusion.

## Historical overview and chronology

Late fourteenth-century humanist study developed through chanceries, schools,
libraries, correspondence, and civic histories. In the early fifteenth century,
Florentine, Venetian, Milanese, Sienese, Ferrarese, Roman, and other workshops
joined contracts, guild practice, patronage, material knowledge, and public or
religious commissions. Brunelleschi, Ghiberti, Donatello, Masaccio, Fra
Angelico, Alberti, booksellers, illuminators, and many collaborators belong to
different evidence settings. The 1453 boundary does not end the Renaissance.

## Evidence and institutions

Manuscripts, letters, civic histories, contracts, account books, guild records,
workshop drawings, buildings, paintings, sculpture, inscriptions, and material
analysis expose commission, collaboration, payment, revision, and reception.
Banking, wool and silk production, Mediterranean trade, inter-city rivalry,
mercenary war, taxation, faction, and territorial expansion shaped the same
urban systems that supported celebrated art and learning.
