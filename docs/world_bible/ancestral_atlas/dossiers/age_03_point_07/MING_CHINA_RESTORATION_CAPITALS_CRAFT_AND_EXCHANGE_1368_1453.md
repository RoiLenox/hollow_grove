# Historical Dossier — Ming China: Restoration, Capitals, Craft, and Exchange, 1368–1453

Status: Reviewed; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

The dossier separates dynastic claims of restoration from the actual work of
officials, examination candidates, military households, eunuch agencies,
artisans, farmers, sailors, merchants, religious communities, and frontier
peoples. Court collections and official histories provide strong but partial
evidence; central policy never proves uniform local implementation.

## Historical overview and chronology

The Hongwu emperor founded the Ming in 1368 and rebuilt central and local
institutions from Nanjing. The 1399–1402 civil war brought the Yongle emperor to
power; Beijing became the primary capital in 1421 after major construction and
canal work. Zheng He’s voyages, court production, examination institutions,
frontier campaigns, and regional markets expanded different networks. The 1449
Tumu crisis exposed military and political vulnerability before the Point’s
1453 close.

## Evidence and institutions

Official histories, codes, gazetteers, maps, examination and court records,
inscriptions, ceramics, paintings, buildings, voyage evidence, archaeology,
and foreign accounts carry distinct provenance. Printing, canals, dikes,
shipyards, porcelain kilns, lacquer, cloisonné, textiles, and architecture
required skilled and coerced labor. Founding war, forced relocation, frontier
violence, hierarchy, and policy reversal remain visible beside craft and
scholarship.
