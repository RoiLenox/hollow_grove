# Historical Dossier — Holy Roman Empire: Electors, Cities, and Councils, 1368–1453

Status: Reviewed; Atlas assignment remains Working.

## Evidence boundary

The dossier separates imperial and civic records from later constitutional
summary. It treats the Empire as overlapping offices and jurisdictions, not a
centralized nation-state, and it does not make imperial or ecclesiastical law
present Hollow Grove authority.

## Historical overview and chronology

The Golden Bull of 1356 remained the procedural background for the supplied
coordinate. Charles IV died in 1378; Wenceslaus, Rupert, Sigismund, Albert II,
and Frederick III followed through contested elective and dynastic processes.
The Councils of Constance and Basel, the condemnation of Jan Hus, and the
Hussite wars show that church reform, Bohemian estates, imperial coordination,
and armed coercion followed separate clocks. Imperial and free cities, princes,
bishoprics, guilds, courts, and villages retained distinct responsibilities.

## Evidence and institutions

Election rules, diplomas, city statutes, guild and council records, council
acts, chronicles, legal manuscripts, seals, coins, buildings, and archaeology
support different levels of claim. Roads, rivers, workshops, walls, mines,
mints, and markets depended on local finance and labor. Feud, crusading,
anti-Jewish violence, taxation, expulsion, siege, and religious persecution
remain consequences and cautions rather than Gifts.

## Modern Hollow Grove manifestation

**Late Medieval Electoral and Civic Register:** a Stonebend provenance system
that separates election, office, forum, principality, bishopric, city, guild,
council, dispute, service, and revision without importing imperial supremacy.
