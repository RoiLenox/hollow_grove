## Chronology decision

The supplied 722–612 BCE range remains a Sequential Point, but its title now reads operationally as apex-to-collapse rather than a uniform imperial zenith. `Judah` is Full / Continues and survives this Point as a kingdom until 586 BCE. `Egypt, Twenty-Fifth and Twenty-Sixth Dynasties` is Dynasty 25 full / terminal; Dynasty 26 partial / continues, because Saite rule begins in 664 while Kushite rule in Egypt ends by 656 and Kushite continuity persists farther south. `Babylon` is Full / Regime-changing / Continues across direct and indirect Assyrian rule, destruction in 689, rebuilding, rebellion, and Nabopolassar's accession in 626. `Neo-Assyria` is Full / Nineveh-terminal; polity continues because the capital falls in 612 while Assyrian kingship and resistance continue at Harran until 609.

The supplied terminal event, Fall of Nineveh, remains unchanged. The dossier layer prevents it from being read as the instantaneous end of all Assyrian political action, population, language, or cultural memory.

## Historical uncertainties

- Judah: the reconstruction of the 701 campaign, Jerusalem's survival, Hezekian and Josianic reform, demographic change, and the dates and layers of biblical composition require comparison among archaeology, inscriptions, Assyrian claims, and later texts.
- Egypt: the 25th/26th Dynasty transition overlaps and is regionally asymmetric. Kushite identity and institutions continue after withdrawal from Egypt; Saite artistic archaism is selective citation, not simple copying or an uncontested national revival.
- Babylon: the label covers multiple rulers and constitutional relationships. Royal destruction and restoration texts are interested claims; temple, scholarly, urban, and political continuity must be tested separately.
- Neo-Assyria: royal inscriptions and palace reliefs privilege court perspectives; letters and provincial evidence reveal uneven capability. Nineveh's fall in 612 is not the same date as the last Assyrian king's resistance at Harran in 609.
