## Historical uncertainties

- New Kingdom Egypt: reign chronology varies; monumental attribution can conceal work communities and earlier phases; Deir el-Medina is exceptional; Dynasty 20 lies mainly beyond the reviewed slice.
- Hittite Empire: royal numbering and absolute dates vary; treaties encode unequal relationships; Hattusa's archive is institutionally selective; successor continuity prevents an extinction narrative.
- Kassite Babylonia: early succession is obscure; Nippur dominates the administrative corpus; Kassite language is sparsely known; kudurrus were generally temple-held monuments rather than field markers.
- Middle Assyria: provincial archives are uneven; administrative categories expose severe coercion; royal claims are adversarial; the state continues after 1200.
- Terminal transition: destruction and abandonment were neither universal nor exactly synchronous, and no single invasion, drought, earthquake, or system model is treated as a complete explanation.
