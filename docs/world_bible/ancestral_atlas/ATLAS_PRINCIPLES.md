# Ancestral Atlas Principles

Status: canonical descriptive framework; assignments remain Working

## Authority order

```text
Hollow Grove Compromise
    ↓
House Constitutions and established canon
    ↓
Ancestral Atlas
    ↓
Hueman manifestations, cultural design, assets, curriculum, and gameplay
```

The Atlas explains historical origin and influence. It never overrides constitutional law, universal laws, established identities, sovereigns, faculties, seasonal functions, geography, runtime behavior, or gameplay rules. A conflict is resolved upward: locked canon wins.

## Data relationship

```text
Historical culture
    ↓
Permanent Gifts
    ↓
House inheritance
    ↓
Modern cultural expression
    ↓
Physical or institutional manifestation
    ↓
Hueman reference
```

An Age is a broad navigation era. Its name is an Atlas coordinate derived mainly from Mediterranean, European, and technological periodization; it is not a claim that every region entered one universal stage simultaneously. A Point is a representative historical coordinate with exactly one primary assignment for each House. Points need not be continuous bins, equally long, or internally synchronous. Documented gaps are transition corridors rather than missing history.

Point types are:

- **Sequential:** the main chronological path;
- **Transition:** a coordinate whose assignments emerge, dissolve, or change form at different dates;
- **Nested:** a simultaneous branch inside another Point or across a boundary;
- **Open:** a present-day coordinate with an `As of` date and scheduled review.

Every assignment records temporal fit and a culture-specific relevance range. **Full**, **Partial**, **Boundary**, **Emergent**, and **Legacy** describe reviewed timing; **Working representative** means the timing has not yet received that depth of review. Primary assignment locates the strongest Atlas inheritance and principal materials for that Point; secondary study is allowed and never duplicates primary ownership.

## Cumulative inheritance

Assignments accumulate. A House does not become a replacement civilization at each Point, and no historical culture literally became a House. Gifts describe useful design inheritances, including remembered failures and ethical cautions; they are not endorsements or exhaustive summaries.

## Translation rule

Influence must pass through Hollow Grove's existing geography, contemporary life, technology, institutions, materials, the universal Oxford/Midnight/Space Cadet foundation, and each House palette. Use design logic—routes, maintenance, proportions, records, gardens, networks, rituals—not uncritical costume or replica districts.

## Status rule

- **Canonical:** use only after explicit ratification.
- **Working:** supplied assignment or preliminary interpretation preserved for review.
- **Needs historical audit:** chronology, evidence, or cultural description needs specialist research.
- **Needs House-fit audit:** chronology may be sound but its primary House assignment needs review.
- **Needs title verification:** supplied book title or attribution is preserved but bibliographic form needs review.
- **Needs date refinement:** a broad Point range obscures a significant chronological boundary.

Working Version 1 explicitly locks no individual assignment. Ages I and II are the more developed Working layers; Ages III–VI receive stronger review flags.

## Research discipline

Point co-placement does not prove direct contact. Do not invent figures, works, doctrines, routes of transmission, or relationships. Unresearched fields remain visible placeholders. Historical corrections require an explicit revision and must not erase the supplied master data.

## Provenance requirement

Every derived design records Point assignments, Gift IDs, modern manifestation, status, and locked canon connection. See [How to Use the Atlas](HOW_TO_USE_THE_ANCESTRAL_ATLAS.md) and the [Provenance Template](schemas/PROVENANCE_TEMPLATE.md).
